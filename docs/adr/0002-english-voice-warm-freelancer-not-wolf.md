# 0002. The English voice is the warm freelancer voice with persuasion structure, not wolf tone

Date: 2026-09-01
Status: accepted

## Context

ADR 0001 chose a full persuasion-first ("wolf") tone for the English edition. Reading the first complete draft, the owner rejected it as arrogant: lecture-like value arguments, challenge lines ("That is the whole argument"), and a swagger that misrepresents who he is. His actual identity: a freelance developer based in Bangkok, Thailand, who takes remote work and builds business websites and back-office systems for customers.

## Decision

We will write the English edition in the same warm, honest, plain first-person voice as the Thai edition, carried by persuasion structure rather than persuasion tone: clear offers, one objection handled at a time, a single email CTA per page. Grade-5 reading level stays. The identity statement "Based in Bangkok, Thailand" appears proudly and plainly. The budget-goes-further argument survives but is stated modestly and sparingly, never as a thesis, never as a challenge to the reader.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep full wolf tone** — the owner read it and rejected it; it sells a person who does not exist.
- **Tone down individual lines only** — the arrogance is structural (thesis-hammering, commands to the reader), so a patch pass would leave the frame intact.

## Consequences

Easier: one voice to maintain across both editions, and the English copy matches the person a client actually meets. Harder: conversion pressure must come from clarity and proof alone. Reversal cost: copy only, no structural work. Supersedes the voice portion of ADR 0001; the rest of 0001 (separate audience, email success event, relaxed jargon, shared hard rules) stands.
